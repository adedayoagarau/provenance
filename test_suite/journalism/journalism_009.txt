Environment: Wildfire Season Analysis

This years wildfire season has broken records that were set only two years ago, with more than 8.7 million acres burned across the western states by early September. Climate scientists point to a convergence of factors: prolonged drought, record high temperatures, decades of fire suppression that have left forests unnaturally dense, and expanding development into fire-prone areas. The economic toll has exceeded an estimated 26 billion dollars, including direct firefighting costs, property losses, and health impacts from smoke exposure affecting urban populations hundreds of miles from the nearest blaze.
